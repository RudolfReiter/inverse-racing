Published at the European Control Conference · ECC 2022

An Inverse Optimal Control Approach for Trajectory Prediction of Autonomous Race Cars

Rudolf Reiter, Florian Messerer, Markus Schratter, Daniel Watzenig, and Moritz Diehl

The paper predicts a race car’s motion by learning the objective behind its driving behavior. It combines a constrained trajectory optimizer with inverse optimal control to estimate acceleration and jerk preferences from observed motion.

Research method

The ECC 2022 paper models an opponent as an optimizer: it seeks progress while balancing motion smoothness and acceleration limits. The inverse problem estimates the optimizer’s parameters from observed motion.

01

Observe vehicle motion

Represent the car by progress along a path, speed, and longitudinal acceleration. Jerk is the control input.

x = [s, v, a],   u = jerk

02

Estimate limits and preferences

Fit an acceleration envelope, then learn two cost weights that explain the measured trajectories.

wa: acceleration   wj: jerk

03

Solve for future motion

Optimize progress and smoothness subject to dynamics, speed bounds, and acceleration constraints.

min −sN + ∑ (waa² + wju²)

Implementation scope. This recreation uses SciPy and nested parameter fitting. The original paper uses a KKT-based inverse formulation and an embedded prediction stack. Read the original method ↗

ADE averages the Euclidean position error at the 30 future states. All predictors start from the same observed state. The constant-velocity baseline follows the same curved path.

The benchmark evaluates forecasts every second. The visual comparison uses nonoverlapping six-second windows for readability. Training and evaluation share the dynamics family and track; the overlapping benchmark windows are correlated.

Learning improves the overall average in this run. Default weights perform better for the balanced driver: fitting observations does not guarantee the best future prediction when the car keeps replanning.

Recreation and limitations

This is a recreated implementation, not the original repository. The original data, KKT-based inverse solver, acados integration, and hardware evaluation are not included. Numerical equivalence to the published results is not claimed.
